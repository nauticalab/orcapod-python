## `src/orcapod/core/function_pod.py`

### F18 — A chained function pod stream was stale right after its first full read
**Status:** resolved
**Severity:** high
**Issue:** ITL-1052

`FunctionPodStream` stamped its modified time when it opened its input
(`_ensure_iterator`). The input of a chained pod stream is a lazy generator, so in
`s2 = g.pod(f.pod(src))` the upstream opened, and stamped, only after `s2` had: `s2` ended its
first full read older than its upstream, and its next read cleared its cache and called `g`
again on every row (6 calls, then 9). Operator streams compute eagerly when they are opened,
so a chain through an operator was not affected; a pod reading a pod directly was.

**Rule** (the same as for job nodes, NS1 / ITL-1042): a stream stamps its modified time when
its results are complete, that is when its input is read to the end, before its reader sees
the end; never when a read starts. `clear_cache()` stamps (a reset is a change). A failed or
partial read does not stamp.
Between a read's first row and its end, the stream is judged by its input's noted state, not
by modified-time order: an upstream completing for the first time during the read is not a
change (F19, ITL-1065).

**Fix:** the stamp moved from `_ensure_iterator` to the end of the input, on the sequential
and the concurrent read paths. A reset of the input during a read must not be reported as
current (the result may mix two states of the input), so:

- each pod stream keeps a reset generation, bumped by `clear_cache()`;
- when the first row arrives (on the concurrent path, once the input has delivered its rows,
  before they are computed), the stream notes its input's state: the generation of a pod
  stream input (whose modified time legitimately moves during the read, when it finishes its
  own read), the modified time of any other input;
- at the end of the input it stamps only if that state is unchanged and the input has a
  modified time; otherwise it clears its own modified time, which makes it stale;
- a stale pod stream reports `last_modified` as `None` (unknown), so every stream reading it,
  however far down the chain, is stale too. `is_stale` compares only direct upstreams, and a
  stale pod stream used to keep its old time: before this fix a reset two links up went
  unnoticed by the last stream, hidden by the false staleness above.

The generation and the noted state are in-memory bookkeeping only: they enter no hash, no
saved file and no database. Regressions: `tests/test_core/function_pod/test_pod_stream_staleness.py`
(chains of two and three, sequential and concurrent, the cached pod stream, a chain through a
join; second reads call no function and read no database; a reset upstream recomputes; a reset
during a read, or made by the function while the stream computes, leaves the reader stale and
its next read is of one state).

Not changed: `FunctionPodStream.as_table` still returns its kept table when the stream is stale
(ITL-1045).
